A morning on the estuary

By Mara Quill, 14 March 2026

Before the tide turns

The mud is still silver when the first walkers reach the sea wall. Oystercatchers work the edge of the channel in pairs, and nobody speaks above a murmur.

What the birds know

By seven the flats are crowded with waders. A curlew drifts in, long bill first, and the smaller birds lift and resettle around it as if on a signal.

The tide tables tell you when the water turns. The birds tell you when it matters.

Walking home

The water comes back quietly, from behind, which is why the path along the wall is the only sensible way home.
